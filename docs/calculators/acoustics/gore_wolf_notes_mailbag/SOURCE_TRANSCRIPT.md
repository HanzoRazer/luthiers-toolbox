**~0:08–0:17 — Question (Bruce, Sydney):** Wolf note on the fifth string — what causes it?

**~0:20–0:36 — Robbie definition:** String frequency same as a guitar natural resonance → clunky/dead sound at that frequency. Introduces Trevor Gore (masterclasses that week).

**~0:54–1:30 — Gore demo (ear):** Wolf sounds distinctly different from notes/strings around it. Open E fine; open A much louder = wolf. A @ 110 Hz matching a guitar resonance. Diagnostic 1: sounds different from neighbors.

**~1:30–2:25 — Tuner diagnostic:** Problems on the A’s. Open E steady on tuner; A on that string plays quite sharp and oscillatory — pushes scale tone out of tune / oscillates.

**~2:27–3:42 — FRF capture:** Visual Analyzer (free). Hammer = bouncy ball + satay stick. Tap lower bout; 10 taps → spectrum. Peaks: main air resonance; main top resonance — awfully close to 110 Hz (A) and 220 Hz (A harmonic). Potential two problems hitting two harmonics of A.

**~4:11–4:31 — Clearance rule:** Move resonances away from scale-note frequencies; typically **half a semitone** away (as far as possible before nearer another scale tone). Alter both air and main top resonances.

**~4:38–5:11 — Air resonance levers:** Soften box (reduce top + back stiffness) → air frequency down. Or change soundhole diameter: smaller → down; larger → up.

**~5:13–5:40 — Top resonance levers:** Add mass → down; reduce stiffness → down; add stiffness → up; reduce mass → up. Choose what suits the job.

**~5:43–6:10 — Close:** Advanced but builders need it. Recommend Gore’s books (search “Trevor Gore book”). Happy building.
