### 2.3 Right frequency relationship → efficiency; miss → wasted energy  
**Timestamps:** ~3:16–4:24  

High-school physics: drive top hard at the wrong relationship → back mostly inert. At the **right frequency relationship**, small drive → large efficiency increase in driving the back member. Miss the resonance relationship → wastes a lot of energy. Takes years sometimes to zero in; serious makers go this direction.

## 4. Somogyi method: stiffness, then brace reduction

### 4.1 Target stiffness via deflection — not target thickness  
**Timestamps:** ~7:14–7:46  

Particular method: take the top down to a certain **target stiffness**. Most people work to target **thickness** — that doesn’t work, because the same thickness of one material translates into varying stiffness in another. All tops to the same [stiffness] via a **deflection test**.

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Classification | **IS** (core method) |
| Link | Apprentice pack **Y03** (stiffness sanding) — same doctrine, apprentice film less explicit on deflection |
| Link to Gore | Overall \(K\) before mobility; different culture (deflection feel vs modal \(Y\)) |
| Toolbox destination | **LAB**/KB — stiffness-gated thicknessing; do not hardcode mm-only defaults |

**Point ID:** ES27
