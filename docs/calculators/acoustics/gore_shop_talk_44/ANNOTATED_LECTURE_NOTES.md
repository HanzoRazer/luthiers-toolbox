### 1.4 Bridge rotation vs top deflection (monopole mobility) — priority  
**Timestamps:** ~16:54–19:27  

Both give rough stiffness info.

**2° bridge rotation rule:** Rough & Ready — under that rotation + normal top radius, avoid **S-shape / roller-coaster** top from string torque. **Do not exceed ~2°.** Many are overly wedded to exactly 2°. CF in bridge/plate reduces rotation (fine).

**Monopole mobility (top deflection jig):** Gore puts **much more credence** here — responsiveness/volume you **hear**. Get mobility **as high as you can**; don’t worry too much about bridge rotation (though both relate to top stiffness).

## 2. Side mass — mechanism (deepest statement yet)

### 2.1 Laminated sides vs tunable sides + side masses  
**Timestamps:** ~22:08–25:16  

Sides more important than most think. Knobs: **stiffness** and **total mass**.

- Laminating: flatter/stabler but hard to vary stiffness afterward; air influence via thickness/side braces limited.  
- **Side-mass blocks:** move **node line of T112 outboard**.

**T112 physics (as stated):** main wood resonance behaves like a **concentric dipole radiator** — in-phase radiation near bridge, **opposite phase** in outer horseshoe → cancellation. Moving node outboard → more positive radiating area, less negative → guitar **quite substantially louder**; also **trims T112 frequency**. Reversible (bolt-on different masses). No real downside stated.

## 3. Live back, free plate, falcate scale

### 3.1 Live / active back  
**Timestamps:** ~26:25–28:38

### 4.6 Scallop vs ramp / X asymmetry  
**Timestamps:** ~50:22–53:29  

Same tonal result often possible either way. Scalloping X tends to return a strongly **asymmetric** structure toward **symmetry**. Plates prefer symmetric modes; forcing asymmetry costs stiffness/mass — then scalloping undoes it (counterintuitive). Falcate’s symmetry is efficient/loud. Scallop vs taper often fiddling forgotten original X intent (e.g. era J-45 diagonal dipole character mentioned).

## 5. Definitions & pedagogy

### 5.1 Wolf tone  
**Timestamps:** ~56:30–58:08  

Overly loud or short note when string frequency matches body mode too closely (fast energy transfer). Avoid by placing body resonances **exactly between** scale tones (**50 cents** off). Other methods exist (not covered).

### 5.2 What is modal tuning?  
**Timestamps:** ~58:57–1:01:08  

Modes = different vibration subdivisions (Chladni shapes at narrow frequency bands). **Modal tuning** = changing the frequencies (Hz / note) at which those modes occur to achieve preferred sound.

### 5.3 What is a “better guitar”?  
**Timestamps:** ~1:01:15–1:05:19  

Three headings (ignore cosmetics):

1. **Playability** — action, shape, frets, physical match to use  
2. **Structural integrity** — stable structure, light panels that still hold, materials/CF as needed  
3. **Musicality** — tone & volume; evenness at high volume; similar notes; **ET intonation**  

Least understood = musicality (course focus). Highly responsive guitars are **harder to intonate** — one reason High Street lacks them. Course: responsive + alluring (strict definition) + in tune.
