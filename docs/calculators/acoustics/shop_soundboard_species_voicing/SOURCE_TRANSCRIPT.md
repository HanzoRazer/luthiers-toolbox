## What a soundboard is (~0:38–1:51)

Top / face plate; speaker prefers **soundboard**. Claims vast majority of acoustic sound from top (~“north of 80%”); back/sides = spices, top = meat.

## Why light / stiff / strong (~1:51–4:33)

Steel flat-top: finite pluck energy, most wasted as heat → top must capture efficiently → lightweight + stiff + strong (goals conflict). Heavy rosewood/ebony/figured maple fail as sails of lead or uneven load paths. Want **straight grain** down the length.

## Book-match + voicing path (~4:39–10:38)

Tops (and backs) almost always book-matched wedges. Glue → thickness → brace → **voice braces** = handmade magic. Factories: fixed thickness (e.g. Sitka **3 mm**) + quick brace chip-down; warranty-driven overbuild. Hand: drum-sand, feel stiffness; Sitka watch zone ~**2.5–2.6 mm**; same-species / same-tree braces when possible; **top deflection under known load** preferred over tap (tap little quantifiable info). Custom can match client climate/use/life of instrument.

## Sitka cases — same species, different wood (~10:50–21:20)

1. Commodity dealer billets (Alaska Specialty Woods etc.) — clean, story unknown.  
2. Bridge reclaimed — metal leach color; decades outdoor flex + humidity cycles.  
3. Ancient Sitka — ASW excavator find; carbon ~3000 yr; glacial mineral color; rare; Collings/SCGC also used; speaker built several; notes repeatable within log.  
4. Torrefied spruce — O₂-starved kiln; stiffer/brittle/stable; antique smell; not snake oil in speaker’s experience.  

Brace wood: match species; prefer same tree; tight parallel grain → lighter braces with strength.

## Don’t buy the species name (~22:05–25:20)

Play every guitar. Factory thickness right for few plates. Elastic Sitka that bends to a circle → speaker scraps (factory would ship). Same log can tap glassy vs cardboard (backs too). Treat each piece as unique.

## Other species brushes (~26:07–31:29)

- European / Carpathian / Italian / German spruce: pale, Sitka-like middle; Alpine slow growth → thinner possible.  
- Adirondack / red spruce: stiffer, often wider grain → louder, more **headroom** (bluegrass).  
- Engelmann: lower headroom / quieter (spoken).  
- Western red cedar: fingerstyle/nylon; softer; often **10–15% thicker** than Sitka; less headroom, wakes easily.  
- Port Orford cedar (cypress / pepperwood): ultra-straight, very stable → travel / plugged-in.

Within-species variables (elevation, butt vs tip elasticity, etc.) still apply. Back/sides ignored in this video (promised later).

## Close (~31:42–33:38)

No definitive wood→result recipe. Builder lets wood guide; buyer listens to the guitar, not headstock/pretty wood alone. Acoustic variables make each instrument different.
