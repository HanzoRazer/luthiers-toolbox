## Thesis (~0:08–1:23)

Most important control for his guitars: thin soundboard to **correct flexibility**. Three deliverables: (1) measure flexibility, (2) thin solid top while monitoring, (3) share his actual target numbers (often secret) as reference points — without numbers, method is “useless.”

## Concepts (~2:01–4:02)

Flexibility before bracing more important than brace pattern/stiffness. Too flexible → bass-heavy/boomy/inarticulate; too stiff → bright/trebly, lack warmth. Same flexibility across spruce vs cedar → remarkably similar finished sound if other treatments match; species differences more damping / speed-of-sound after attack (player-subtle).

## Why deflection (~4:13–5:54)

Thickness ≠ stiffness (boards differ; with-grain vs cross-grain ratios differ). Thin to flexibility correlated with past good guitars; iterative sneak-up. Final thickness secondary (ballpark for next tops).

## Demo plate (~5:54–6:53)

Solid spruce demo (he usually builds double tops); density **0.40 g/cm³**; prefab rosette; thick/stiff start.

## Deflection method (~6:53–8:10)

Hang as bridge over fixed span; dial indicator under mid-span; uniform weight; measure with-grain and cross-grain. No universal spans — use **same format every time**, record, transpose others’ formats by recreating theirs then yours.

## Rig & geometry (~8:16–13:38)

MDF table with aluminum slats; dial through table. Simple triangles in bench corner also OK. With-grain: interest below upper transverse bar (upper bout widest → 10 mm inside final tail edge); dial at mid-span; **+4 mm** cutoverhang on tops. Cross-grain: slats 10 mm inside widest upper & lower bouts. Weights: with-grain **2 lb** load, preload **180 g** Ibex; cross-grain load **180 g**, preload **30 g**. Cross-grain always much more flexible → lighter load.

## Measure / thin loop (~13:45–33:10)

Scorecard: weight, thickness, tap, both δ. Preload → zero dial → gentle load. Initial example: with-grain **0.106"**, cross-grain **0.116"** (too stiff). Targets on screen (±10% band); intended with **28-ft** lower-bout radius + conventional fan bracing; bridge later raises cross-grain stiffness a lot. Drum-sand uniform first (sneak; 0.1 mm passes change a lot); then preferential palm-sand (220) lower bout / waist to drop with-grain more than cross-grain. Stop when feel + δ + tap agree.

## Physics of thinning (~24:10–27:18)

Stiffness loss ≫ linear thickness loss; rectangular beam ~ thickness³ (example: −4% t → predict −12% k; measured −21% with-grain — ballpark). Cross-grain even more sensitive. Stiffness drops faster than weight → pitch ∝ √(k/m) family falls. Rules of thumb: −10% t → ~−27% k; −50% t → ~−88% k.

## Thickness map outcome (~33:31–35:09)

Non-uniform map: preserve **central peninsula** in lower bout (bridge stress). From video still / map: center ~**2.3 mm**, rings ~2.2 / 2.1 / 2.0 / 1.9, periphery ~**1.8 mm**. With-grain stiffness dominates structure (string pull); cross-grain can be quite flexible for air pump / volume / low resonances.

## Forward path (~35:45–38:06)

Later: brace + dome rebuilds stiffness; carve braces to resonances before glue to sides; may use cross-grain bridge bar if cross δ overshot. Solid tops can still thin from outside after boxed. Ep. 14 = double tops.
