## Materials & aging (~12:01–14:20)

**Q — Torrefied spruce:** Pre-ages wood → more brittle, stiffer, less dense; harder to glue / glue failures. Gore does not use it; plain wood gives plenty good enough response.

**Q — Guitars sound better over time?** Papers / long-term tests (Gore recalled UNSW). Short term: rapid change in first minutes under tension (usually only luthier hears); stabilizes ~1 week; then changes slow drastically.

## Nut compensation (~18:30–21:21)

Beneficial for equal temperament. Even with correct open + 12th-fret intonation, frets 1–2–3 tend sharp: wide low-fret spacing → more deflection when fretting → sharp. Pressing lighter isn’t a solution (unclean notes).

Moving nut toward first fret shortens string → drop tension to restore open pitch → **affects all frets**, not only the first. Nut + saddle compensation → very small error. Hard to go back after playing a properly compensated guitar.

---

## Elevated board / tilt neck (~21:41–22:37)

No falcate classical with elevated fingerboard. Small-body classicals with **tilt neck** (adjustable via wheel through soundhole) are not dissimilar in purpose.

---

## Chladni symmetry (~22:42–24:08)

Patterns need **not** be symmetrical. Asymmetric bracing (Martin X; fan) yields asymmetric Chladni. Easiest path to symmetry: symmetric bracing — don’t fight the structure.

---

## SRC vs frequency analysis (~24:10–25:20)

Chat clarified **sound radiation coefficient**. Gore answered the correlated question: Chladni strong-pattern frequencies should match FRF peaks from tap testing — **yes, strongly**.

## Bridge-off measurements & mobility (~25:26–26:06)

Measuring FRF / monopole mobility on closed box without bridge: **significant** change when bridge added — adds stiffness and mass. Monopole mobility ~ **1/√(k·m)**.

---

## “Way more than four main modes” / spectral character (~26:19–29:07)

Interpreted as strong response in main low modes vs highs. Lattice often little upper FRF activity → “vanilla”; can be intentional (e.g. Williams / Smallman). For more “alluring” sound from the guitar, want more peaks up to ~1 kHz. **Live back** helps produce more peaks ~300–1000 Hz → more tone; absorbs some top energy → slight volume loss. Non-live → more volume.

Pointers to *Contemporary Acoustic Guitar Design and Build* Vol 1 (theory, live/non-live backs) and Vol 2 (build to acoustic specification).

---

## Bridge rotation 1.5° → 2° (~30:10–31:57)

First: what does it sound like? Priorities: (1) resonances in right places, not on scale tones; (2) high monopole mobility; (3) compensation/intonation. Don’t obsess 1.5 vs 2° — you don’t hear that. If too stiff: lower brace height (fastest); CF-capped braces make that hard.

---

## Free-top tuning (~32:24–35:21)

Yes — gluing to sides changes the note; bridge changes more; closed box couples top/air/back/sides. Free-top tap tuning pointless as finished proxy unless receiving assembly is identical every time (impractical). Tune in **design**, then **closed-box** techniques from the book.

---

## Neck heavy vs light/stiff (~36:36–37:35)

Adding bolt-on tilt neck doesn’t seem to alter body resonances much. Neck modal frequencies don’t dominate body resonances. Strength under pull + correct geometry matter more than tuning neck to a frequency.

---

## Why asymmetric X bracing (~37:41–39:52)

Martin popularized asymmetric X for steel strings; liked and copied. Symmetric designs sound different. Falcate principally symmetric. X tends to suppress cross-dipole, cross-tripole, long-dipole → thins mids → scooped mids (broad brush). Symmetric / falcate puts mids back; flatter FR more classical-like.

## Archtop falcate (~40:12–41:05)

Gore doesn’t make archtops; vibration principles transfer; archtop builders have found the books useful.

---

## Isolate live back to lower bout? (~42:53–43:37)

With his standard back bracing, upper bout doesn’t move much; hasn’t needed special measures. If upper back interferes, stiffen it.

---

## Resonances off scale tones / wolves (~43:42–45:13)

Resonance on scale tone → wolf: high admittance drains string energy → loud short note, uneven. Avoid by tuning resonances **midway between** scale tones.

---

## Shop demo — Visual Analyzer FRF (~46:19–52:12)

- App: Visual Analyzer (setup on website/book/course); early version preferred.  
- Capture spectrum; tap ~10 times; inspect to ~1 kHz; watch “grass.”  
- Peaks on falcate body **without bridge:** air ~102 Hz; main top T(1,1)₂ ~192 Hz; back-coupled T(1,1)₃ ~226 Hz.  
- Bridge expected to drop T(1,1)₂ ~10–12 Hz → ~180 Hz region.  
- Want ~4 semitone separation between that peak and the next → ~226 Hz.  
- Cross-coupling: lowering top peak also pulls air peak down (toward ~100).  
- “Good numbers” nearly mid-scale: **100 / 180 / 226** — taste-dependent but nearly ideal for this box; should hit without drastic post trim.

Software notes (~56:07–58:12): Silent Soft Visual Analyzer; Gore uses v9.0.6; Windows; Mac limited (Audacity weaker); Wine/emulator workaround.

## Cross-dipole / tripole targets (~1:00:15–1:01:17)

Gore tends not to target specific cross-dipole/tripole frequencies on steel strings — consequence of bracing; rarely wolf problems. Classical: first-string support often wants a resonance (cross-tripole especially) in first-string frequency range.

## Poster putty = bridge mass? (~1:01:23–1:02:06)

**No.** Glued bridge adds stiffness + mass. Putty/taped bridge adds mass only (no rigid stiffness coupling).
