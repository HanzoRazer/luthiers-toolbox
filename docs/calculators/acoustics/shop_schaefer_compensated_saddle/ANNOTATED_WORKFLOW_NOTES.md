## 2. Slot slant vs saddle shape (don’t conflate)

### 2.1 Steel slanted slot ≠ “fully compensated saddle”  
**Timestamps:** ~2:07–3:33  

Steel fretting sharpens more than nylon (modulus / proportional gauge effect) → slanted saddle *slot*. Classical slot ~straight; nylon needs little compensation. Separate from bone shape.

**Point ID:** SC03 · **EP**  

### 2.2 Topic is break-point geometry on the saddle blank, not slot angle  
**Timestamps:** ~3:35–3:50  

**Point ID:** SC04 · **EP** / **ESF**

## 8. Harsh truths (setup anti-tail-chase)

### 8.1 Human pitch discrimination ~2–5 cents (average musician)  
**Timestamps:** ~20:27–21:50  

100 cents = 1 semitone. Don’t design for sub-2¢ perfection.

**Point ID:** SC22 · **EP** / **ESF**  

### 8.2 Fretted instruments are inherent intonation compromises  
**Timestamps:** ~22:02–22:14  

**Point ID:** SC23 · **EP**  

### 8.3 Primary check: 12th harmonic (or open) vs fretted 12th  
**Timestamps:** ~22:15–22:41  

Mid-board spot-check only for local defects (e.g. flat fret).

**Point ID:** SC24 · **EP** / **ESF**  

### 8.4 Perfect 12th ≠ perfect mid-board (variable fretting stretch + rise)  
**Timestamps:** ~23:02–24:41  

String must rise toward saddle or elliptical motion buzzes frets behind.

**Point ID:** SC25 · **EP**  

### 8.5 True Temperament frets illustrate “perfect everywhere” cost  
**Timestamps:** ~24:59–26:54  

Squiggly CNC frets (Sweden) — real product; not required. Shows straight-fret limits.

**Point ID:** SC26 · **EO** / **OH** / **ESF**  

### 8.6 Don’t chase every mid-fret G into every other G  
**Timestamps:** ~26:18–26:54  

Get close; respect ear limits.

**Point ID:** SC27 · **EP** / **ESF**  

### 8.7 Note envelope: prefer starting transient over decay on strobe  
**Timestamps:** ~27:07–29:29  

Transient and decay can disagree in pitch; don’t force both.

**Point ID:** SC28 · **EP** / **ESF**
