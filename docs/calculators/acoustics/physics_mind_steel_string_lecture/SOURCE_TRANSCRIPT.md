## Framing (~0:00–2:30)

Physics gives a **language** for what the maker already does by intuition and empirical tradition — not a formula-first build method. Analogy: “what is it like to see red?” vs wavelength description. Caveat: he does **not** disassemble and retune every part to fixed resonances then reassemble; builds by feel, then can quantify afterward.

## String / body energy (~2:30–6:10)

Strings put energy into the body. A lone string moves little air. The guitar is **not** an amplifier — it **wastes** much of the string energy budget; makers try to use what remains efficiently. Whole instrument vibrates (neck, back); also bellows / air-pump motion; radiation from surfaces **and** soundhole. Pluck position changes harmonic mix (center = rounder fundamental; near bridge = more overtones). Harmonic spectra give instrument identity (blind violin demo).

## Bridge / saddle (~6:09–6:48)

Energy transfer at **bridge + saddle** (bone on quality instruments). Strings tug with their spectra; body responds with **its** modal preferences — spectra are the available energy budget, not a forced body spectrum.

## Wood movement & selection (~6:55–13:15)

Wood moves with humidity; retune instruments on humid days. Cups away from pith; prefer **quarter-sawn** (moves in/out, less cup/bow). Economics drive poor commercial cuts; hunt stacks for usable planks. Straight grain rare; southern-hemisphere twist anti-clockwise following sun; tropical woods often reverse-twist → straighter usable grain. Prefer wood from **split billets** following natural split lines. Select by **stiffness-to-weight** and fist-tap sustain/resonance (and crack check) — physical properties over historic reputation. Spruce (Sitka/Engelmann/Alpine): premier top S/W despite softness. Western red cedar: warmer, easier bass, lower-tension instruments. Backs: mahogany (Brazilian CITES / scarce), rosewood (Brazilian CITES 1972; speaker avoids BR risk; twisted set discarded). Local reclaim / Perth wood shows.

## Anatomy / insides (~16:30–20:40)

Outside appearance ≠ what makes sound; under-hood bracing is critical. Live demo: unfinished top braces being carved toward tap tone; rectangular back braces still tap; kerfed linings nearly through-cut by hand; brace ends into linings matter; Venetian cutaway allows camera + late brace adjust after cut.

## Soundboard bracing & modes (~20:46–29:10)

Steel ~**700 N** (~70 kg) on bridge for life of guitar. Braces not strictly required for a short-lived box, but without them: poor sound + collapse. Primary job: **control vibration modes**; secondary: long-term stability. **Monopole** (in/out bellows / Helmholtz radiation): critical for steel-string **bass**. **Cross dipole** (bridge teeter side-to-side): classical/nylon treble + projection. **Long dipole** (bridge rock fore-aft): hard to enhance without weakening structure. Modes mix; higher orders (2–4 nodes) → chaotic top motion; bracing is non-homogeneous control. Nylon schemes run long braces for cross dipole (incl. Esperance lattice maker). Speaker realm: **X-brace** steel; bridge (external) ties X legs for monopole; small X-angle changes bias long/cross dipoles; finger + lower transverse braces shape spectrum. Small cedar body: omit second finger brace, one transverse, open lower bout for monopole/bass. **Scalloping myth:** factory overbuild → plane through soundhole; better: slim taper correctly first.

## Cube rule / domes / Chladni (~29:13–35:30)

Brace width ~linear stiffness; height ~**cube** stiffness — easy to over-thin; can’t put wood back. Domed tops/backs (violin carved dome analogy): speaker uses ~**40 ft** top / ~**28 ft** back radius dishes + go-bar deck → can lighten braces toward mode-control roles. Flat tops also work but need more structural bracing. **Chladni** (UNSW images): sand nodes on free top; interesting; speaker personally uninterested as process; free top ≠ rim-fixed response. Patterns grow complex with frequency (~77 Hz near low E; high E noted).

## Linings / back (~35:31–41:40)

Santa Cruz (Richard Hoover) tip: **basswood** linings — no preferred cleavage plane → isolate top/back from sides so plates act more like a drum; tune back and top to each other. Mahogany linings couple sides (player hears more locally). Rejects side **sound ports** (aesthetics). Back role: trampoline analogy — in sync enhances sustain; out of sync can stop motion. Player test: tap/play against body vs off body. Stand-up strap players may not use back → don’t over-invest acoustically. Fingerstyle / on-knee: design live back in (e.g. Tasmanian blackwood). Back bracing aims at **long dipole**; small bodies → 3 braces not 4; carve for resonance.

## Scale / historic / intonation (~41:43–48:55)

Historic short-scale gut/oud-like instruments quiet (small rooms). Modern steel: common **25.4"** (Martin) and **~24.9"** (Gibson). Same strings: longer scale → more tension, more fundamental/bass energy into top, slightly higher action clearance; short scale → weaker bass, sweeter treble. Long-scale tambura demo (inefficient pumpkin body still sustains). **Intonation:** thick strings stiff → effective vibrating length shorter at ends → slant/carve saddle by measurement (strobe tuner: open vs octave / chord positions), not slavish factory copy. Setup of cheap copies is common paid work.

## Bridge placement / loading / finish (~48:55–55:15)

Bridge is most important (visible) brace: height, weight, wood, flex. Best placement **center of body** for monopole (music-box demo: center loudest; edge tinny). Short children’s guitars forced off-center by hand size / balance — known inefficient bass. Stretching neck/scale to recenter bridge = intentional design graph. High saddle = loud short-term, risks top buckle; relief slots dump force without excessive saddle height. Finish: protect moisture swings; **thin** modern lacquer; thick factory coats kill tone; French polish thinnest/best acoustically but fragile (alcohol/heat).

## Q&A (~55:41–59:50)

Adhesives: aliphatic resin + **hide glue** on repairable joints (both stronger than wood). Age/break-in: real; EM string-vibrator product for break-in; play them. Resonator/slide first guitar: aluminum diaphragm. Non-diatonic fretting: slide yes; fretted usually Western diatonic. Electrics: wood still matters (body/neck/board); plastics nasal. Cutaway: upper-fret access; upper bout relatively inactive; slight air-volume / Helmholtz effect — acceptable trade.
