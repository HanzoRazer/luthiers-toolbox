## Bracing purpose (~0:27–0:50)

Braces/struts: thin QS or split wood (usually spruce) glued inside top to (1) withstand string pull, (2) modify top stiffness for tone, (3) sometimes help provide arch to the top.

## Three historical types (~0:57–4:45)

1. **Ladder** — earliest; braces across body width (top and back); lute/cittern and early gut guitars; lower tension → more prominent overtones; also parlor steel (small body/short scale); simplicity → Sears/Roebuck mass production → folk/blues prevalence.  
2. **Fan** — Spanish mid-18th c.; 3→5→7 fans; **Torres** (b. 1817) refined large arched soundboard, seven fan braces + two diagonal lower-bout braces; warmer, stronger bass, broader dynamics → concert template extinct small concert bodies after early 1850s.  
3. **X** — attributed to **C.F. Martin** refined ~1840–1845 (invention uncertain); trained ladder under Stauffer; after 1833 NY immigration responded to US market; tried fan then settled X; by 1850s most of his guitars X-braced. Today most steel-string tops use X or variant.
