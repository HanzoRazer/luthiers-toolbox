## Problem / brace families (~0:30–5:49)

Shows his evolving steel X; contrasts classical **fan**. Guitar inefficient vs violin (finite pluck energy vs continuous bow). Braces stiffen ~2 mm top against bridge torque (bridge wants down, top sink). Steel ≈ **twice** the pull of nylon → need much stiffer top → X ties whole top at main pressure point; fan leaves top freer (OK for nylon, too loose for steel long-term).

## Contradictory goal + mass (~5:56–6:45)

Want top to move (vibrate) but not collapse. Lighter top → easier to start moving → remove mass where possible.

## Cube / height rule + I-beam idea (~6:50–9:32)

Flat spruce bends easily; upright (tall) much stiffer (~“ten times” spoken — qualitative). Neutral axis: bottom stretches, top compresses, mid little stressed → mass near mid less valuable. Construction **I-beam**: flanges take compression/tension; web keeps them apart; more height → stiffer.

## Acrylic deflection demos (~9:45–16:05)

Homogeneous acrylic braces in stiffness jig + weight:

| Specimen | Approx size | Deflection (spoken) | Mass (spoken) |
|----------|-------------|---------------------|---------------|
| Square | 10×10 mm | ~22–23 mm (either orientation) | 44.4 g |
| Taller square | 10×12 mm (+20% height) | ~11 mm | — |
| Triangle (pyramid carve, same H×W as stronger square) | same H×W as 12 mm square? / “same height and width” as square then carved | ~22 mm | 31.7 g |
| I-beam (same outer H×W as solid square) | — | ~14 mm | 26.7 g |

Spoken rule of thumb: **+20% height → ~2× stiffness** (half deflection). Triangle loses ~half stiffness vs solid square. I-beam ≈ solid stiffness (14 vs 11 mm) at ~half mass (26.7 vs 44.4 g); lighter than triangle with far more stiffness than triangle.

## Spruce / balsa experiments (~16:13–20:32)

Spruce I-beams ~21 g, defl ~5–5.5 mm; leave middle material for X join — little stiffness cost. Balsa-core + spruce skins ~16.3 g, ~6.5 mm. Must keep separator connected in height direction; sideways sandwich fails. Tapered “normal guitar brace” shape ~6.6 g, ~10 mm — much less stiff than I-beams. Mass/stiffness most needed near center; toward ends height matters less.

## Applied X top (~20:45–25:40)

I-beam almost all braces; remove mid mass; leave top flange; scallops / gaps so regions can rock (bass wants whole top move flat-ish) while X still ties structure. Center of guitar = energy dump → laminated spruce patch joining X legs (not just thin spruce patch over gap). Tap: loud/responsive; bass side looser/more area, treble side closer/smaller braces → brighter. Will string in guitar later. Teases soundhole physics video.
