## How a guitar works (~2:27–5:24)

**GN:** Energy from strings → soundboard; large vibrating surface amplifies. Solid-body lacks that acoustic projection. Acoustic difference = resonances from soundboard **and** air volume in the box.

Speed of sound depends on density and stiffness of the medium: air ~**343 m/s**; typical spruce soundboard ~**4,500–5,000 m/s**; beryllium (Focal tweeter membranes) ~**12,000 m/s** (very light + stiff).

Stradivari lore: tap one end of a log, apprentice listens other end for transmission delay — speed of sound still useful for how mechanical waves transmit in materials.

Wave path: strings → saddle → bridge → soundboard — already bone/ebony or rosewood + spruce; each material junction alters the traveling wave. At soundboard edges: how much energy transfers to sides/back vs remains in soundboard → **sustain**. Complexity/uniqueness of acoustic-guitar sound.

## Responsiveness (~5:38–7:50)

Responsive guitar (Somogyi books ~2009 referenced by host). **GN:** Responsiveness = most important parameter — **dynamic character**: sensitivity to light finger touch **and** headroom under stronger strumming.

Factory instruments often less responsive: industry prioritizes reliability (returns, neck resets). Increasing responsiveness also risks more **wolf tones** and strange resonances.

Responsiveness directly related to **equivalent mass** of the soundboard — the part that vibrates and produces sound (not the whole board). Upper bout / neck–headblock region usually not very active. **Monopole** centered around the bridge (heaviest element) defines **mobility** and hence responsiveness.

## Guitar vs Hi-Fi / studio monitor (~7:50–10:15)

Loudspeaker industry uses drivers in linear cone-motion range. Acoustic guitar imagined as a **distortion producer**. Monopole ≈ pistonic up/down — like Somogyi’s **pump**. Also dipoles (long/cross), tripoles, multipoles → richness; each mode = FRF peak. Technically that peaked/colored behavior = “distortion” vs studio monitor’s flat linear I/O (discourages added harmonics). Guitar musicality = interaction of those harmonics; builders must tune resonances onto **right spots** for harmonic sound.

## Sustain (~10:27–11:15)

Sustain = property of resonances: how long for energy to be absorbed after impulse (pluck). Not only fundamental — also **harmonic/overtone sustain**. Different frequency behavior changes perceived sound type.

## Monopole + External Tuning Slot (ETS) (~11:36–12:49)

Monopole = most important single resonance; frequency + amplitude define tonal balance, dynamic character, and whether the guitar suits the player.

Drum analogy: soundboard = skin; sides/back = shell. Changing shell stiffness/mass shifts skin main resonance — same idea. **ETS (External Tuning Slot)** at center of lower bout: accessible slot with changeable mass weights to tune main soundboard resonance frequency. Small mass changes can make a big overall sound difference.

## Wolf tones (~12:56–14:14)

Wolf: note disappears quickly — strong interaction when played note fights instrument resonance (e.g. monopole). Sounds out of tune / unclean; differs from other fretboard notes. Higher mobility/sensitivity → more wolf exposure. Research focus: build systems that minimize interference between soundboard resonances and fretboard notes.

## TPC tonewood measurement system (~14:34–17:18)

**TPC** system designed to measure tonewood: density, radiation coefficient, especially **damping** (historically hard to measure accurately). Jig supports sample on **nodal lines**; ramp drops **marbles** onto a specific point for consistent tap amplitude; mic underneath records impulse; custom software processes → resonance frequency, damping factor, stiffness, radiation coefficient. Usually two taps; select impulse; select frequency; select central decay portion → calculate. Record library for wood selection/comparison over years. Software buyers get jig plans, 3D ramp drawings (print), and marble.

## Offset soundhole (~17:30–19:06)

Acoustic: traditional hole sits where board still active — moving it frees radiating area. Structural/sustainability: move hole off axis of ~**70 kg** continuous string tension → more stable, less bellying, better acoustic-part behavior. Player: slightly closer but not port-level blast toward player; seek balance across bandwidth.

## Orchestra model neck/body design (~19:06–22:00)

13-fret join; overlapping neck without heel directly on headblock → single-piece whole neck wood, long-term stability; avoid classic 14-fret “ski jump”; lower action possible; bolt-on serviceability. Soundboard curves gracefully into upper bout — dome/curve increases rigidity vs flat; organic one-piece look. Not archtop: flattop style with **radius** lower bout + **curved** upper bout.

## Woods: Macassar ebony back + Alpine spruce (~22:11–26:30)

Cameroon Macassar ebony B&S: acoustic + aesthetic + sustainability. **Active back** (at least orchestra model) — midrange timber richness. Density: if active-back density too **low**, back resonates too much, **absorbs** energy from soundboard → lose projection/clarity. Mobility applies to back as well as top; slightly higher back mass lowers back mobility to the “right spot.” (Host misconception corrected: denser ≠ more “reflective” in the naive sense — here about controlling back mobility.)

Alpine spruce from Jura; long relationship with Les Bois de Lutherie (historic French sawmill context) + APG (French guitar luthier association). Local/sustainable/emotional attachment. With clear sound targets can compensate mechanical/tonal properties across woods up to a point — not cloning one perfect guitar.

Knowledge helps players understand issues/preferences — not a parameter set that fully defines sound.

## Joining the dots — D28 vs Larrivée example (~26:38–29:01)

Book section **“joining the dots”**: measurements of historic models (Martin D28, OM-28, etc. — one specific type). Example: Martin D28 vs Larrivée (spoken “alar”/“lar”) — same dreadnought shape, Sitka, ebony/rosewood bridge, Indian rosewood B&S — but parameters/sound differ:
- **D28:** very responsive, dynamic, punchy, projecting mid-range (higher monopole mobility)
- **Larrivée:** much lower monopole mobility (higher soundboard mass) — smooth, low-end shift, big voice, few/no wolves across fretboard, less projecting

Same owner loves both — not better/worse; understand qualities.
