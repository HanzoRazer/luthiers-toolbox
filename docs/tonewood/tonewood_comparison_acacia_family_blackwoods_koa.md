# Tonewood Comparison: Acacia-Albizia Family — Blackwoods, Koa, Monkeypod, and Relatives

> **Source:** `wood_species.json` v4.0.0 + `tonewood_viability_emerging_species.md`
> **Generated:** 2026-06-22

This comparison covers the Acacia and Albizia genera tonewoods — closely related Fabaceae that share the "warm, balanced" tonal signature. Includes Hawaiian Koa, Tasmanian/Malaysian/Australian Blackwood, Monkeypod, Mugavu, and African relatives. These are the woods Rory McGuinness described as having "that fundamental and very direct powerful tone... but with more warmth behind it" compared to African Blackwood (a Dalbergia, not an Acacia).

---

## Nomenclature Clarification

The trade names are confusing. Here's what's actually what:

| Trade Name | Scientific Name | Region | Notes |
|------------|-----------------|--------|-------|
| **Tasmanian Blackwood** | *Acacia melanoxylon* | Tasmania, SE Australia | The canonical species |
| **Malaysian Blackwood** | *Acacia melanoxylon* | Planted in Malaysia | Same species, different origin |
| **Australian Blackwood** | *Acacia melanoxylon* (likely) | Mainland Australia | Same species, regional variant |
| **Hawaiian Koa** | *Acacia koa* | Hawaii | Endemic cousin species |
| **Mangium** | *Acacia mangium* | SE Asia, Pacific | Plantation timber, lighter |

**Key insight:** Tasmanian, Malaysian, and Australian Blackwood are all *Acacia melanoxylon*. They are the same species grown in different regions. The database lists them separately because sourcing region can affect density and figure.

### Property Analysis

**Density cluster:** All four species fall within ±10% of 615 kg/m³. This is the "warm tonewood" density band — heavier than spruce/cedar tops (350–450), similar to mahogany (530–590), lighter than rosewood (800–850).

**Hardness divergence:** Koa and Tasmanian Blackwood are nearly identical (~1,165 lbf). Australian Blackwood and Mangium are significantly softer (467–552 lbf). This may reflect:
- Regional growing conditions
- Age at harvest
- Data sourcing variation

**Stiffness (MOE):** Australian Blackwood leads at 14.82 GPa despite lower hardness — unusual. This suggests higher specific modulus (E/ρ), which correlates with "quicker" response.

### Acoustic Interpretation

**Specific modulus ranking:**
1. Australian Blackwood (23.2) — highest stiffness-to-weight ratio
2. Mangium (18.9)
3. Tasmanian Blackwood (18.5)
4. Hawaiian Koa (16.6)

Higher specific modulus typically correlates with:
- Faster attack response
- Stronger projection
- Clearer note separation

**Acoustic impedance:** All four are in the 2.5–3.1 range — moderate impedance woods that transmit energy efficiently without excessive reflection. Compare to African Blackwood at ~4.8 (high reflector) or spruce at ~1.5 (low impedance, high transmission).

## The "Malaysian Blackwood" Question

Rory McGuinness described Malaysian Blackwood as having:

> "that fundamental and very direct powerful tone to it... but it has more warmth behind it"

compared to African Blackwood's tendency to become:

> "almost too steely"

**The physics confirms this:**

| Property | African Blackwood | Malaysian/Tasmanian Blackwood |
|----------|:-----------------:|:-----------------------------:|
| Density (kg/m³) | 1,270 | 640 |
| Acoustic Impedance | ~4.78 | ~2.75 |
| Damping | Very low | Moderate |
| Overtone Retention | Strong | Attenuated |
| Fundamental Emphasis | Less relative to overtones | More prominent |

Malaysian Blackwood is simply *Acacia melanoxylon* grown in Malaysia. The tonal character Rory describes — powerful fundamental with warmth — is characteristic of the entire *A. melanoxylon* / *A. koa* family, not a unique property of Malaysian-sourced wood.

## Koa vs Blackwood: Are They Interchangeable?

The database describes Tasmanian Blackwood as a "premium koa alternative at lower cost." The physical data supports this:

| Property | Hawaiian Koa | Tasmanian Blackwood | Difference |
|----------|:---:|:---:|:---:|
| Density (kg/m³) | 625 | 640 | +2.4% |
| Janka (lbf) | 1,170 | 1,160 | -0.9% |
| MOE (GPa) | 10.37 | 11.82 | +14% |
| Tone Character | "warm, balanced, opens up with age like rosewood" | "warm, balanced, opens up with age — koa alternative" | — |

**Key differences:**
- Koa has higher tearout tendency (0.50 vs 0.30) due to more figured grain
- Blackwood has slightly higher stiffness (11.82 vs 10.37 GPa)
- Both described with identical tone character language

**Practical implication:** For builders priced out of koa, Tasmanian Blackwood offers nearly identical acoustic properties at a fraction of the cost with easier machining.

## Mangium: The Plantation Alternative

Mangium (*Acacia mangium*) is widely planted in Southeast Asia and the Pacific as a fast-growing timber crop. Its properties position it as a potential entry-level Acacia tonewood:

| Comparison | Mangium vs Tasmanian Blackwood |
|------------|-------------------------------|
| Density | 8.6% lighter (585 vs 640) |
| Hardness | 60% softer (467 vs 1,160 lbf) |
| Stiffness | 6.3% lower MOE |
| Machining | Significantly easier |
| Guitar Relevance | Exploratory vs Established |

**Assessment:** Mangium's low hardness (467 lbf) makes it unsuitable for fretboards or bridges, but it could work for body construction if the lower hardness doesn't cause denting issues. The acoustic properties (similar specific modulus, similar impedance) suggest it would be in the same tonal family. Currently unexplored as a tonewood — a gap waiting to be filled.

## Summary: The Acacia Tonewood Family

All four species share:
- Moderate density (585–640 kg/m³)
- Moderate acoustic impedance (2.5–3.1)
- "Warm, balanced" tone character
- Good machinability vs harder woods

The family contrasts with African Blackwood (Dalbergia) on every axis:

| Axis | Acacia Family | African Blackwood |
|------|:-------------:|:-----------------:|
| Density | ~620 kg/m³ | 1,270 kg/m³ |
| Impedance | ~2.7 | ~4.8 |
| Damping | Moderate | Very low |
| Tone | Warm, fundamental-forward | Glassy, overtone-rich |
| Typical Use | Body, back/sides | Fretboard, bridge, wind instruments |

**The builder's choice:** If you want warmth and balance with strong fundamentals, the Acacia family delivers. If you want cutting projection and overtone complexity, look to the Dalbergia rosewoods or the extreme-density woods like African Blackwood and ebonies.

### Monkeypod: The Luthier's Favorite

**Bob Taylor:** *"I love monkey pod"*

Monkeypod has the strongest luthier endorsement of any species in this family:

| Rating (Tonewood Data Source) | Score |
|-------------------------------|:-----:|
| **Overall Tone** | 4.0 / 5 |
| **Bass** | 4.4 / 5 (rich harmonics, strong presence) |
| **Mids** | Warm, smooth |
| **Trebles** | Mellow, intimate |
| **Projection** | Below average ("not loud") |
| **Sustain** | Below average vs rosewood/maple |

**Tonal positioning:** "Between Koa and Mahogany" — the low MOE (7.92 GPa) creates the warm, mellow character, not a structural defect.

**Best applications:** Back/sides (acoustic), body (electric/acoustic), neck — anywhere warm Koa-like tone is desired.

**Blocking issue:** Supply chain, not stiffness. Bob Taylor's only complaint.

---

### Mugavu: The Strategic Opportunity

Mugavu fills the density gap between Sapele (640 kg/m³) and Bubinga (890 kg/m³):

| Comparison | Mugavu Position |
|------------|-----------------|
| Density | 764 — heavier than Sapele, lighter than Bubinga |
| Stiffness | ~14.5 GPa — stiffer than Monkeypod (7.92), approaching Bubinga |
| Impedance | ~3.33 — higher than Monkeypod (2.18), approaching Ovangkol (3.88) |
| Supply | Abundant East African; furniture export channels exist |
| CITES | Not listed |

**Blocking issue:** ALL acoustic data is estimated. No measured tap-tone, no Western builder trials documented.

**Recommendation:** High-priority prototype candidate. Same genus as Monkeypod but denser/stiffer voice expected.

### Monkeythorn: The Southern African Wild Card

Monkeythorn (formerly *Acacia galpinii*, now *Senegalia galpinii*) is a heavyweight in this family:

| Property | Monkeythorn | vs Koa | vs Mugavu |
|----------|:-----------:|:------:|:---------:|
| Density (kg/m³) | 800 | +28% | +5% |
| MOE (GPa) | 13.14 | +27% | -9% |
| Impedance | 3.24 | +28% | -3% |
| Janka (lbf) | 834 | -29% | -54% |

**Anomaly:** Despite high density (800 kg/m³), Monkeythorn has relatively low Janka hardness (834 lbf) — softer than Koa (1,170) despite being 28% denser. This unusual profile suggests:
- Possibly more compliant cell structure
- May machine easier than density suggests
- Acoustic character may differ from density prediction

**Status:** Unexplored as tonewood. No lutherie characterization in database.
