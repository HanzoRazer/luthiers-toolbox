# Tonewood Comparison: Extreme Density Woods — The "Glassy Character" Spectrum

> **Source:** `wood_species.json` v4.0.0
> **Generated:** 2026-06-22

This comparison catalogs woods in the African Blackwood density class (≥950 kg/m³) — the extreme-density species that produce "glassy," "steely," or "bell-like" tonal character due to high acoustic impedance and low internal damping.

---

## What Creates "Glassy" Tone?

When builders describe tone as:
```
glassy, steely, bell-like, piano-like, crystalline
```

They are hearing the acoustic signature of:
- **Very high density** (≥1,000 kg/m³)
- **Very low internal damping** — energy stays in vibration longer
- **High acoustic impedance** — reflects energy rather than absorbing
- **Strong overtone retention** — upper harmonics persist

These are the woods that "ring" when tapped — the marimba, clarinet, and high-end fretboard woods.

## Tier 1: Primary Tonewoods (Known, Characterized)

These are the benchmark "glassy" woods — the species all others are compared against.

| Species | Density | Janka | MOE | Tone Character | Typical Uses |
|---------|:-------:|:-----:|:---:|----------------|--------------|
| **Macassar Ebony** | 1,120 | 3,220 | — | Bright, defined | Fretboard, bridge, decorative |
| **Cocobolo** | 1,095 | 2,960 | — | Warm, rich, complex — among finest rosewoods | Body top, back/sides, fretboard, bridge |
| **African Ebony** | 1,030 | 3,080 | — | Bright, articulate, percussive | Fretboard, bridge, nuts |

### Notes

**Macassar Ebony** (*Diospyros celebica*): The hardest wood in the registry (Janka 3,220). Striped figuring. Indonesian origin. Vulnerable conservation status.

**Cocobolo** (*Dalbergia retusa*): The only rosewood that reaches this density class. Exceptional tonal complexity — "warm" despite high density due to oil content affecting damping. CITES Appendix II.

**African Ebony** (*Diospyros crassiflora*): The traditional benchmark ebony. "Bright, articulate, percussive" — the glassy signature. Endangered; Taylor Guitars promotes streaked alternatives.

### Notes

**Ipe** (*Handroanthus spp.*): Hardest wood in this comparison (Janka 3,510). "Bell-like, extreme sustain" — the ultimate glassy character. South American. Commonly used for decking; lutherie use is secondary.

**Katalox** (*Swartzia cubensis*): Often marketed as "Mexican Ebony" despite being unrelated to *Diospyros*. The hardest of all (Janka 3,650). Central American. Not CITES-listed — a viable ebony alternative.

### Notable Emerging Species

**African Blackwood** (*Dalbergia melanoxylon*): The densest true rosewood. Traditional clarinet/oboe wood. "Glassy, steely, focused" character. CITES Appendix II. The benchmark for this entire comparison.

**Snakewood** (*Brosimum guianense*): Exceptionally high MOE (23.20 GPa) — the stiffest emerging species. Distinctive snakeskin figuring. Extremely expensive. Used for violin bows, not guitars.

**Greenheart** (*Chlorocardium rodiei*): Highest MOE in the emerging tier (24.64 GPa). Guyanese. Exceptional stiffness-to-weight ratio. Used in marine applications; lutherie potential unexplored.

**Mopane** (*Colophospermum mopane*): Southern African. Lower MOE (13.22) despite high density — may have different acoustic character (more damped?). Used in African instrument making.

**Texas Ebony** (*Ebenopsis ebano*): North American. Not a true ebony (*Diospyros*) but earns the name from dense dark heartwood. The only extreme-density domestic US species. No CITES restrictions.

## Summary

### The Glassy Character Spectrum

| Density Band | Species Count | Character |
|--------------|:-------------:|-----------|
| **Ultra-dense (≥1,200)** | 8 | Extreme impedance; maximum overtone retention |
| **Very dense (1,100–1,199)** | 12 | Traditional ebony/rosewood range |
| **Dense (1,000–1,099)** | 28 | African Blackwood class |
| **High (950–999)** | 17 | Threshold of glassy character |

### Key Findings

1. **65 species** exist in the African Blackwood density class (≥950 kg/m³)
2. Only **5 are established tonewoods** (3 primary + 2 established)
3. **17 are emerging** with growing builder adoption
4. **43 are completely unexplored** — massive opportunity space

### The Opportunity

The database reveals that extreme-density tonewoods are **severely under-characterized**. For every African Ebony or Cocobolo, there are 8-10 unexplored species with similar or superior physical properties.

**Highest-priority candidates for characterization:**
1. **Greenheart** — highest MOE (24.64), should outperform ebony
2. **Australian Buloke** — sustainable domestic alternative
3. **Texas Ebony** — US domestic, no restrictions
4. **Gidgee** — Australian Acacia, very dense

The "African Blackwood class" is not a single species — it's a physical property band with dozens of potential members worldwide.
