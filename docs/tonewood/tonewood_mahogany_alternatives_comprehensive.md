> **Purpose:** Deep-dive alternatives beyond "the usual suspects"

This document goes beyond Sapele and Khaya to catalog **1,740 CIRAD species** and emerging alternatives that luthiers are actually discovering.

## The Problem with "Usual Suspects"

Most mahogany-alternative guides list the same 5-6 species:
- Sapele
- Khaya (African Mahogany)
- Nato
- Meranti

These are fine woods, but they represent **less than 1%** of available species in the mahogany density range (450–700 kg/m³). CIRAD documents **1,740 species** in this band across **45+ families**.

---

## Part I: The True Mahogany Family (Meliaceae)

### What Makes a "True" Mahogany?

Only the genus *Swietenia* is true mahogany:
- *Swietenia mahagoni* — Cuban/West Indian Mahogany (CITES II, essentially unavailable)
- *Swietenia macrophylla* — Honduran/Big-leaf Mahogany (CITES II, regulated)

Everything else marketed as "mahogany" is a relative or substitute.

### The Entandrophragma Genus (The Real Alternatives)

These are the closest genetic relatives to true mahogany, all from the Meliaceae family:

| Species | Trade Name | D12 | Specimens | Origin | Notes |
|---------|-----------|:---:|:---------:|--------|-------|
| *Entandrophragma cylindricum* | **Sapele** | 0.66–0.70 | 200+ | West/Central Africa | Industry standard; slightly harder than mahogany; ribbon figure |
| *Entandrophragma utile* | **Sipo/Utile** | 0.67 | 39 | West/Central Africa | **Closest to true mahogany** per timber industry; more uniform |
| *Entandrophragma candollei* | **Kosipo** | 0.68–0.70 | 50+ | West/Central Africa | Heavier than Sapele; less figure |
| *Entandrophragma angolense* | **Tiama/Gedu Nohor** | 0.55–0.60 | — | West Africa | Lighter; more like Cuban mahogany |
| *Entandrophragma excelsum* | — | 0.50 | — | Burundi | Lightweight variant |

**Key insight:** Sipo (*E. utile*) is considered by the timber industry to have "the most similar tonal qualities to genuine Mahogany" — yet it's rarely discussed in guitar contexts. Sapele gets all the attention because it's more available.

### The Carapa Genus (Andiroba/Crabwood)

Underexplored genus with **excellent density match**:

| Species | D12 | Specimens | Origin | Notes |
|---------|:---:|:---------:|--------|-------|
| *Carapa guianensis* | 0.65–0.70 | 150+ | Brazil, French Guiana | **Andiroba** — massive specimen count; very consistent |
| *Carapa procera* | 0.67 | 24 | French Guiana | Similar to above |

**Why this matters:** Carapa has more CIRAD specimens than any other Meliaceae genus except Entandrophragma. It's well-characterized, consistent, and available — yet rarely discussed for guitars.

## Part II: Emerging Industry Alternatives

### Taylor's Urban Wood Initiative

Taylor Guitars has pioneered urban salvage wood:

| Wood | Density | Source | Tone Character |
|------|:-------:|--------|----------------|
| **Urban Ash (Shamel Ash)** | ~550 | Southern California city trees | Bold midrange, strong fundamental — **comparable to Honduran mahogany** |
| **Urban Ironbark** | ~1,100 | California Eucalyptus | Bright, dense — ebony-adjacent |

**Why it matters:** Shamel Ash (*Fraxinus uhdei*) is a Central American species planted throughout California since the 1940s. Trees are now reaching end-of-life and being removed — Taylor sources from arborists. The wood rivals mahogany tonally but with zero import issues.

### Sapele: Beyond "Just a Substitute"

Sapele has evolved from budget alternative to respected tonewood:

> "Sapele, once considered just a mahogany substitute, is now recognized for its slightly brighter tone with beautiful overtones... strong low-end and midrange bark, very similar to mahogany, but delivers more top-end definition."

| Property | Honduran Mahogany | Sapele |
|----------|:-----------------:|:------:|
| Density (kg/m³) | 545 | 640 |
| Janka (lbf) | 800 | 1,360 |
| Character | Warm, balanced | Warm + bright top-end |
| Sustainability | CITES II | Protected harvest |

### Okoume/Ayous: The Lightweight Revolution

| Property | Value | Notes |
|----------|:-----:|-------|
| Trade Names | Okoume, Ayous, Abachi, African Whitewood | Same species: *Aucoumea klaineana* |
| Density | 380–430 kg/m³ | Lighter than basswood |
| Character | Mahogany-like but brighter | Similar to Korina |
| Users | PRS, Shergold, Burns (1970s) | Historically "budget" — now reconsidered |

**CIRAD data:** D12 0.49–0.50 with excellent consistency across 50+ specimens from Gabon/Congo.

### Paulownia: The Ultralight Contender

| Property | Value |
|----------|:-----:|
| Density | 280–320 kg/m³ |
| Character | Between basswood and alder; mellower than ash |
| Growth | 12 years to maturity (vs 50+ for ash) |
| Users | Fender Brad Paisley model, Suhr |
| Issues | Soft; dents easily |

**Market position:** Paulownia finished bodies can weigh under 2 lbs — a pound less than equivalent swamp ash. Not CITES listed; extremely sustainable.

## Part III: Domestic (US) Alternatives

### The Catalpa Option

*Catalpa speciosa* — Northern Catalpa / Cigar Tree

| Property | Value | Source |
|----------|:-----:|--------|
| Density | ~420 kg/m³ | Estimated |
| Character | Clear, bright, pronounced fundamentals, few overtones | Luthier reports |
| Workability | Excellent; lightweight | Forum consensus |
| Availability | Common ornamental; large specimens | Eastern/Central US |

**Builder feedback:** "Catalpa has been discussed as a replacement for Honduras Mahogany as a neck wood... very resonant... lighter than mahogany or ash."

### Sassafras

*Sassafras albidum*

| Property | Value |
|----------|:-----:|
| Density | ~480 kg/m³ |
| Character | Similar to Catalpa and Swamp Ash |
| Notable Use | Fender Eric Johnson "Virginia" Strat |
| Workability | Excellent — cuts, machines, sands nicely |
| Availability | Eastern US; finding large pieces is challenging |

### American Sycamore / Lacewood

*Platanus occidentalis* (US) / *Platanus × acerifolia* (London Plane)

| Property | Value |
|----------|:-----:|
| Density | 545 kg/m³ |
| MOE | 9.79 GPa |
| Character | Warm, open, airy; pronounced mids |
| Special Feature | Quartersawn shows dramatic "lacewood" ray fleck |

**Note:** "Sycamore" in Europe means maple (*Acer pseudoplatanus*). American sycamore is *Platanus* — completely different genus.

### Black Locust

*Robinia pseudoacacia*

| Property | Value |
|----------|:-----:|
| Density | 770 kg/m³ |
| Janka | 1,700 lbf |
| Character | Bright, focused |
| Advantage | Extremely rot-resistant; domestic; abundant |

Denser than typical mahogany but domestic and sustainable.

## Summary

**1,740 CIRAD species** exist in the mahogany range. The guitar industry uses perhaps 10.

**Key findings:**

1. **Sipo** (*Entandrophragma utile*) is considered closer to true mahogany than Sapele — yet Sapele dominates because it's more available
2. **Andiroba** (*Carapa guianensis*) has 150+ CIRAD specimens with excellent consistency — essentially unknown in lutherie
3. **Urban Ash** is Taylor-validated as mahogany-comparable — sourced from city trees in California
4. **Paulownia** is revolutionizing lightweight builds — 12-year growth cycle, not CITES listed
5. The **Burseraceae family** (copal/incense trees) is completely unexplored despite 88 species in range
6. **Domestic US options** exist: Catalpa, Sassafras, Black Locust, Sycamore

The "mahogany shortage" is partly a failure of exploration. The alternatives exist — they just haven't been characterized.
