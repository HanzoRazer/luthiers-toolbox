---

## Builder Context: What Creates "Glassy" Tone?

> "African Blackwood... can become almost too steely"
> — Rory McGuinness, luthier

When builders describe tone as **glassy, steely, bell-like, piano-like**, they hear:

| Acoustic Mechanism | Physical Cause | Result |
|--------------------|----------------|--------|
| Strong overtone retention | Very low internal damping | Harmonics persist longer |
| High-frequency reflection | High acoustic impedance (Z = ρc) | Energy reflects at boundaries |
| Articulate attack | High stiffness (MOE) | Quick response, note separation |
| Extreme sustain | Low energy absorption | Notes ring, don't decay |

**The density threshold:** Woods above ~1,000 kg/m³ typically exhibit these characteristics. Woods above ~1,200 kg/m³ are extreme — the clarinet/oboe/marimba woods.

## Summary

**50 species** in the database exceed 1,000 kg/m³. Only **5 are established tonewoods**. The remaining 45 represent massive untapped potential.

**Key findings:**
1. **Greenheart** (MOE 24.64) and **Wamara** (MOE 24.38) are stiffer than any characterized tonewood — they should theoretically outperform ebony for energy transfer
2. **Australian species** (Buloke, Gidgee, Waddywood) offer sustainable domestic alternatives to African woods
3. **Black Ironwood** is a Florida/Caribbean option denser than African Blackwood
4. The **primary tonewoods** (Macassar Ebony, Cocobolo, African Ebony) are missing MOE data — a critical gap for acoustic modeling

The "African Blackwood class" is not a single species — it's a physical property band with dozens of potential members worldwide.
